RWE is one of the world's leading players in the offshore wind sector and has more than 20 years of experience in the development, construction and efficient operation of offshore wind farms. We operate 18 offshore wind farms and are currently constructing new projects in Europe with a total capacity of over 4 GW. Our construction projects include the Sofia wind farm (1.4 GW) off the British coast, the Thor offshore wind farm (1.1 GW) in Denmark, the Nordseecluster (1.6 GW) north of the German island of Juist and the OranjeWind offshore wind farm (795 MW) in the Netherlands, which we are realising together with TotalEnergies. Further offshore wind farms are under development worldwide. RWE has set itself the goal of expanding its global offshore wind capacity to 10 GW by the year 2030. The focus for further growth is on attractive markets in Europe and the Asia-Pacific region.

A strong tailwind for the energy transition

Strong wind is one of the most powerful natural forces on planet Earth – and it is thus also a powerful source for generating climate-friendly energy. The principle is that wind gusts set the three rotor blades in motion, which in turn drive a generator, the electricity from which is fed directly into the grid.

In many countries, wind power is already making an important contribution towards the generation of electricity from renewables, and the numbers are rising. We are therefore proud to be one of the world’s largest owners and operators of onshore and offshore wind farms.

And we want to keep on growing in this dynamic market – both alone and in collaboration with partners.

In the future, Offshore Wind Farms are to be connected to each other with the help of interconnectors. These cross-border power lines link Offshore Wind Farms to national and international power grids. This promotes the integration and distribution of wind power and strengthens the security of supply in Europe.

The power of wind

Offshore wind farms make use of stable wind conditions and the high wind speeds at sea.

Out in the open sea, the wind turbines are anchored to the seabed commonly using fixed bottom foundations.

Floating foundations are required for sea depth of 60 meters or more.

How the electricity comes ashore

Offshore wind farms usually have their own substation where all produced electricity is fed into. Some have their own converter station at sea.

From the sea, the electricity is then transported to the onshore substation where it can be fed into the public grid.

Rotor blade recycling

As a pioneer in offshore wind energy, RWE continues to focus on sustainable innovation and plays a key role in advancing the development of recyclable rotor blades.

RWE is installing recyclable rotor blades from Siemens Gamesa on the majority of turbines at the Sofia offshore wind farm, marking the first use of this recyclable composite technology in the UK following its successful deployment at the Kaskasi wind farm in Germany. The rotor blades used, manufactured by Siemens Gamesa, are made from a unique resin that allows for easy separation and recycling of the materials at the end of their life cycle – for example, in vehicle parts or consumer goods. This creates high-quality recycling opportunities.
